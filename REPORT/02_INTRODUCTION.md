\# 1. INTRODUCTION



\## 1.1 Background



Batten Disease, also known as Neuronal Ceroid Lipofuscinosis (NCL), refers to a group of rare inherited neurodegenerative disorders. NCL disorders are associated with progressive neurological deterioration and can involve different genetic subtypes, including CLN1, CLN2 and CLN3.



The clinical presentation of NCL can vary between patients and across disease subtypes. Because Batten Disease is rare and clinically heterogeneous, its evaluation can require the consideration of multiple sources of clinical and imaging information.



Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) have increasingly been applied to healthcare-related data analysis. Machine learning techniques can identify patterns in structured clinical data, while deep learning methods, particularly convolutional neural networks, can learn visual representations directly from medical images.



This creates an opportunity to investigate AI-based approaches for supporting the analysis of Batten Disease-related clinical and MRI information.



---



\## 1.2 Batten Disease and NCL Subtypes



Neuronal Ceroid Lipofuscinoses comprise a group of inherited neurodegenerative disorders associated with progressive neurological impairment.



Different genetic forms of NCL are associated with different clinical characteristics and disease courses. In this project, clinical and mutation information associated with:



\- CLN1

\- CLN2

\- CLN3



is used for clinical machine learning.



The clinical component of the proposed system is therefore designed to predict the NCL subtype rather than directly produce a clinically calibrated probability of Batten Disease.



---



\## 1.3 Role of Artificial Intelligence



Artificial Intelligence provides computational methods for identifying patterns within large or complex datasets.



Machine learning can be applied to structured clinical information to identify relationships between patient characteristics and disease subtypes.



Deep learning can process image data using neural networks that automatically learn hierarchical visual features.



In medical imaging applications, deep learning models can therefore be investigated for classification and image-based pattern recognition.
